## Air filter in Tucson

An air filter, called a furnace filter or particulate filter, is a porous medium installed inside an HVAC system that captures airborne particles, including dust, pollen, pet dander, mold spores and fine debris, before they circulate through the ductwork and into living spaces. Air filters are a type of HVAC component, sitting at the return-air side of the system where all indoor air passes through before reaching the blower and coil. ### Parts of an air filter

An air filter is made up of five core parts: the filter media, the frame, the pleats, the gasket and the support mesh. The **filter media** is the material that does the actual capturing, and it comes in two main types: pleated synthetic and fiberglass. **Pleats** increase the surface area of the media without increasing the frame size, which raises capture efficiency without choking airflow. The **frame** holds the media rigid so air cannot bypass the edges, and the **gasket** seals the filter against the slot to prevent unfiltered airflow from slipping around the sides. The **support mesh** keeps the media from collapsing under the pressure difference between the dirty and clean sides.

### Measurable attributes of an air filter

Air filters carry several measurable attributes that determine how well they perform in a given system. The MERV rating is the primary measure of filtration efficiency. It runs from 1 to 16 on the standard residential scale: a MERV 8 filter captures most pollen and dust mite debris, while a MERV 13 filter captures particles as small as 0.3 micrometers, including fine smoke and some bacteria. Particle capture efficiency is expressed as a percentage across a defined size range, typically 0.3 to 10 micrometers. Pressure drop, measured in inches of water column, describes how much resistance the filter adds to airflow. A higher MERV rating generally raises pressure drop, which is why matching the filter to the system's airflow capacity in CFM matters. Dimensions are stated in inches and must match the filter slot exactly, since a loose fit allows unfiltered air to bypass the media entirely. Media type affects both efficiency and longevity. Pleated synthetic media typically lasts 60 to 90 days, while basic fiberglass media is usually replaced every 30 days.

### How an air filter connects to the broader HVAC system

An air filter is part of the HVAC system and works directly with the air handler, which draws return air through the filter before conditioning and distributing it. A clean filter protects the evaporator coil from dust accumulation; a dirty evaporator coil, the direct result of a clogged or missing filter, loses heat-transfer efficiency and can freeze over, stopping cooling entirely. The filter protects the blower motor and heat exchanger from debris that otherwise coats their surfaces and reduces output. ### MERV rating and air filter replacement intervals

Air filter replacement is the single most frequent HVAC maintenance task, and the correct interval depends on MERV rating, household conditions and local air quality. A MERV 1 to 4 filter in a lightly occupied home may last up to 90 days, while a MERV 11 to 13 filter in a home with pets and high dust load often needs replacement every 30 to 45 days. A dirty air filter raises system static pressure, reduces airflow in CFM and forces the compressor to run longer cycles to reach the set temperature. ### Signs of a dirty air filter

A dirty air filter produces observable signs before it causes equipment damage. Reduced airflow from supply registers is usually the first indicator. The system then runs longer than normal to reach the thermostat setting, and dust accumulates on supply grilles while a musty or stale odor emerges from the vents. In severe cases, the evaporator coil freezes entirely. When that occurs, the system stops producing cold air. A direct consequence of restricted airflow through a clogged filter. Checking the filter visually every 30 days takes seconds: if the media is gray and the pleats are packed with debris, replace it immediately rather than waiting for the next scheduled service. ### Where air filters apply in residential and light commercial systems

Air filters apply in every forced-air HVAC system, including central air conditioners, heat pumps, furnaces, packaged units and ductless mini split systems that include a washable media panel. In residential settings, the filter typically sits at the return-air grille or inside the air handler cabinet. Light commercial systems often use larger filter banks with multiple filters in parallel to handle higher airflow capacity in CFM without excessive pressure drop. Ductless systems use a washable particulate filter inside each indoor head rather than a disposable furnace filter, and those panels need rinsing every two to four weeks in dusty environments.
